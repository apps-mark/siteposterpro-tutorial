Site Poster Pro turns a batch of TXT articles into a complete Google Site: one site with one page per article. Google Sites has no publishing API, so the work runs in your own Chrome browser through the **GSite Tier Publisher** extension. When the site is published, the extension sends every page URL back to Site Poster Pro.

## Good to know first

- **Chrome only.** Publishing runs through a Chrome extension, in your own browser. Keep Chrome open until the process is finished.
- **No images.** Google Sites pages are created with text and links only.
- **No connected accounts.** Google Sites does not use the accounts from Connections. You choose the Google account in the extension each time.
- **No scheduling.** A Google Site is created and published in one run.

## Before you start

- **Google Chrome** with the **GSite Tier Publisher** extension installed. Use the install link in Site Poster Pro. Version **4.72.3 or newer** is required.
- A **Google account** that you want to create the site with.
- Your articles as **TXT files with HTML**. Each file becomes one page: the first `<h1>` becomes the page title. Files without an H1 are blocked. See [TXT import format](/content/txt-import-format/).

## Create your Google Site

<Steps>

1. **Open TXT Import and select Google Sites** under *Publish to*.

2. **Assign a client (optional).**
   The client is only used to label the result in your overviews. Client connections are not used for Google Sites.

3. **Enter the Google Site title.**
   This becomes the name of the site. If the name is already taken on that Google account, Google Sites adds a number to it automatically.

4. **Upload your TXT files.** Every file becomes one page of the site.

5. **Click Create Google Site.**
   Site Poster Pro prepares the publishing job and connects to the extension. You see a message like *GSite Tier Publisher connected, 9 pages ready*.

   Not ready to publish yet? Click **Save Draft** instead. You can load the batch later with **Load Saved Draft**, on any device where you're signed in.

6. **Open the GSite Tier Publisher extension** in Chrome.
   It shows **Connected**, the site name, the number of pages and *Locked to Tier 1*. If it doesn't show your job yet, click **Get next publishing job**.

7. **Click Choose Google Account** and pick the Google account for this site.

8. **Click Open Google Sites & start.**
   The extension opens Google Sites and creates the site and its pages automatically.

</Steps>

## Let it finish

<Aside type="caution" title="Don't click Publish yourself">
Let the extension run until the end. Don't click anything in Google Sites while it is working, and never click **Publish** manually. The extension publishes the site itself and then reads the page URLs. If you interfere, the URLs may not come back to Site Poster Pro.
</Aside>

A few pages are done in a minute or two. Larger sites take longer: with **30 to 60 pages**, Google Sites itself gets slower as the site grows. Keep the tab open and wait.

## Get your URLs

When the site is published, the extension sends all page URLs back to Site Poster Pro. You see a confirmation that the site was published and how many page URLs were saved.

Open [Published URLs](/results/published-urls/), select **Google Sites** and use **Copy all URLs** to copy every page URL at once.

## Troubleshooting

**"Chrome Extension did not confirm the connection."**
The extension is not installed, not enabled or the page needs a refresh. Install or enable GSite Tier Publisher, refresh Site Poster Pro and click **Create Google Site** again.

**"GSite Tier Publisher v… is outdated."**
Your extension version is too old. Update it to 4.72.3 or newer, reload the extension in `chrome://extensions` and refresh Site Poster Pro.

**The extension doesn't show my job.**
Click **Get next publishing job** in the extension.

**The site was published, but the URLs are missing in Site Poster Pro.**
Open the extension and click **Retry published page URLs (no republish)**. It reads the URLs again without publishing the site a second time.

**The site name has a number at the end.**
That name was already used on the Google account. Google Sites adds the number automatically. This is normal.

**It takes a long time.**
Larger sites take longer because Google Sites slows down as pages are added. Let it run and don't close the tab.
