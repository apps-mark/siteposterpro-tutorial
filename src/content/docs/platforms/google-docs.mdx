Site Poster Pro turns every TXT article into a Google Doc in your own Google account. You choose whether the documents stay **private** or are **published to the web**, so they get a public URL that search engines can find. You can create documents right away or schedule a batch that Site Poster Pro creates and publishes for you.

## What gets created

- **One Google Doc per TXT file.** The first `<h1>` becomes the document title and heading.
- **Formatting is kept:** paragraphs, headings, bold text and hyperlinks. Keyword links from your TXT files are added too.
- **No images.** Google Docs are created with text and links only.
- Documents are created in the Google account you connect. Site Poster Pro can only access the documents it creates itself, not the rest of your Google Drive.

## Before you start

- A **Google account** for the documents.
- Your articles as **TXT files with HTML**. Files without an H1 are blocked. See [TXT import format](/content/txt-import-format/).

You don't need a Google Cloud project or API key.

## Create Google Docs now

<Steps>

1. **Open TXT Import and select Google Docs** under *Publish to*.

2. **Select the client** (optional). The documents are labelled with that client in Published URLs and the calendar.

3. **Upload your TXT files.**

4. **Click Connect Google Docs**, choose your Google account and approve access.
   You see *Google Docs connected* with your email address.

5. **Choose private or public.**
   - Leave **Publish to web automatically after creating each Doc** **off** to create private documents. They are not publicly accessible and won't be indexed.
   - Turn it **on** to publish every document to the web, so it gets a public URL anyone can open.

6. **Click Create Google Docs** (or **Create & Publish to Web**).
   Site Poster Pro shows the progress per document. Each document gets an *Open Google Doc* link and, when published, its **public Publish to web URL**.

</Steps>

<Aside type="note" title="Public means verified">
A public URL is only saved after Site Poster Pro has checked that the document really opens **without a Google login**. Results show *Published to web · Anonymous access verified*. You don't need to check it in an incognito window yourself.
</Aside>

### Publish a private document later

Created a document privately and want it public after all? In the results, click **Publish to Web via Google API** next to that document.

If Google blocks the automatic publication, the document shows **Manual publishing needed**. Then:

1. Open the document and choose **File → Share → Publish to web** in Google Docs.
2. Copy the public URL. It ends in `/pub`.
3. Paste it into the field next to the document and click **Verify & Save public URL**.

## Schedule Google Docs

The scheduler creates and publishes the documents at their planned times, **even when your browser is closed**. It needs its own one-time authorization.

<Steps>

1. **Upload your TXT files** in TXT Import with Google Docs selected, and select the client.
   You can schedule **up to 15 files per batch**.

2. **Click Connect Scheduler (offline)** and approve access with your Google account.
   You see *Offline Google Docs authorization saved for* your email address.

3. **Under Google Docs Scheduler, set:**
   - **Offline Google account**: the account to create the documents in;
   - **First publish date & time**, in your local time;
   - **Interval**: the time between documents, in minutes, hours or days.

4. **Click Schedule & Publish Google Docs.**
   The documents appear in the queue with their planned times.

</Steps>

<Aside type="caution" title="Scheduled documents are always public">
Scheduled documents are **published to the web automatically**. They are not created before their scheduled time, so you won't see them in Google Drive until then.
</Aside>

Good to know about the queue:

- Site Poster Pro checks the queue **every five minutes**, so a document can appear a few minutes after its exact time. Busy queues may take longer.
- Changed your mind? Click **Cancel** next to a scheduled document before it is created.
- Use **Refresh queue** to update the list and **Show completed history** to see published documents.

## Find your URLs

All documents are saved under [Published URLs](/results/published-urls/):

- **Published** documents with their public `/pub` URL;
- **Created** (private) documents with their edit link.

Use **Copy URLs** in the results to copy all links at once. Public URLs are used when they are verified.

Created documents in an earlier session that aren't listed? Connect Google Docs and click **Sync existing Docs to Published URLs**. This finds the documents Site Poster Pro created in your Google account and adds their edit links.

You can also see scheduled and published documents in the [Publishing Calendar](/scheduling/publishing-calendar/). Select the client and the week to view them.

## Troubleshooting

**"Reconnect your Google account and retry."**
Your Google sign-in has expired. It lasts about an hour. Click **Reconnect / switch Google account** and try again. Documents that were already created are kept and won't be created twice.

**"Manual publishing needed."**
Google did not allow the automatic publication, or the public check failed. Use **Publish to Web via Google API** again, or publish manually via File → Share → Publish to web and paste the `/pub` URL.

**"Paste the public Google Docs Publish to web URL (ending in /pub)."**
You pasted the private `/edit` link. Use the URL from File → Share → Publish to web instead.

**"Choose 1–15 valid TXT files to schedule."**
The scheduler takes at most 15 files per batch. Split larger batches.

**"Connect Scheduler (offline) and select a Google account first."**
The scheduler needs its own authorization. Click **Connect Scheduler (offline)**.

**"Select a start date and time at least one minute in the future."**
Pick a later start time.

**A scheduled document is a few minutes late.**
That's normal. The queue is checked every five minutes.

**Start over with another Google account.**
Click **Reset Google Docs**. This clears the form and disconnects the Google accounts, including the scheduler. Published URLs and completed history stay saved. Cancel scheduled documents first.
